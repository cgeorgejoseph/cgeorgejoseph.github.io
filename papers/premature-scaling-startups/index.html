Why do startup founders scale too early?

Entrepreneurial cognition and premature scaling of startups: a qualitative analysis of determinants of start-up failures

George Joseph, Nimitha Aboobaker, Zakkariya K.A. · Journal of Entrepreneurship in Emerging Economies, 15(1), 96-112, 2023

Emerald Literati Outstanding Paper Award, 2024

What the paper found

Premature scaling is traced here to how entrepreneurs think and feel when they decide, not only to the business idea. Six patterns came out of the interviews: comparison with others, emotional over-reaction, impatience, mistaken customer priorities, overestimation and overconfidence.

Key findings

  • Comparison: founders who keep measuring themselves against competitors drift from their own path and justify decisions after the fact.
  • Emotional over-reaction: when frustration drives a founder's decisions, the same pattern spreads through the team.
  • Impatience: wanting growth faster than the business can bear leads to hasty conclusions and short-run trade-offs.
  • Mistaken priorities: scaling on thin information about the market gap or what customers actually need.
  • Overestimation: emotional commitment produces over-optimism, with effort going into acquiring new users before existing ones are satisfied.
  • Overconfidence: attending only to evidence that confirms the idea and ignoring information that contradicts it.

What it means in practice

The paper argues for attention to the person as much as the concept: aspiring entrepreneurs need to learn to assess themselves and others objectively, seek advice and think strategically before scaling.

How the study was done

In-depth semi-structured interviews, each 25 to 50 minutes, with eight participants reached through purposive and snowball sampling, analysed thematically around the question of how premature scaling mistakes happen. The paper reads the results through self-regulation attitude theory.

Where it is relevant

Useful for work on startup failure, entrepreneurial cognition, decision-making heuristics, cognitive bias in founders and the sustainable growth of technology startups.
