Where does ethical reasoning about health-care AI actually happen in public?

Where health-care AI ethics becomes public: documentation as ethics infrastructure in open-source repositories

George Joseph · Digital Policy, Regulation and Governance, ahead of print, 2026

What the paper found

Public ethical deliberation about open-source AI is uneven, and the paper argues that it depends on scaffolding: paid maintainer time, contributor pathways and communication channels. Two concepts follow. Documentation as ethics infrastructure names the guides, example notebooks and terminology discussions through which ethical reasoning becomes visible. The translation gap names what is lost when reasoning done thoroughly upstream reaches health-care organisations only as a label.

How the study was done

A comparative case study of four GitHub repositories (Fairlearn, AIF360, ClinicalBERT and BioBERT) from January 2020 to January 2025, drawing on 450 keyword-retained issues and 2,136 comments.

Where it is relevant

Useful for work on AI governance, responsible AI, fairness toolkits, clinical language models, open-source communities and how health-care organisations procure, validate and oversee AI components.
